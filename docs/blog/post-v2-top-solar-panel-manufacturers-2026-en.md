## Why 2026 Changed Solar Procurement Rules {#why-2026-changed}

For most of the 2020s, the solar panel market operated on a straightforward logic: manufacturers scaled aggressively, prices fell consistently, and buyers benefited from both. Procurement was primarily a price optimization exercise.

That model has fractured — not collapsed, but fundamentally changed in four ways:

### The Technology Divergence Problem

TOPCon has become the mainstream n-type production route, displacing PERC at scale. But BC (Back Contact) and HJT (Heterojunction Technology) have moved from niche to viable alternatives for specific project types. This creates a genuine decision point: you are now choosing a technology route, not just a brand.

The implications are significant:
- **BC modules** (LONGi's pathway) offer the highest efficiency in real-world conditions, with reduced shading losses due to rear-contact cell design
- **HJT modules** (available from Risen, Astronergy, and others) have superior bifacial performance and lower temperature coefficients — critical for high-irradiance climates
- **TOPCon n-type** (dominant across most of the top 10) delivers the best cost-per-watt with good efficiency, making it the rational default for most utility and C&I projects

### The Financial Stability Dimension

Scale does not equal solvency. A manufacturer that ships 80GW per year but operates at thin or negative margins represents a different risk profile for a 25-year warranty than one shipping 20GW with strong EBITDA. Procurement teams are now modeling counterparty risk into supplier selection — not just price per watt.

### Price Stability Over Price Level

European market prices moved noticeably above previous lows in early 2026. The procurement implication: buyers who locked in long-term framework agreements in 2025 are now better positioned than spot buyers. In 2026, price stability — the predictability of future pricing — is becoming as important as the price level itself.

### Regional Supply Capability as a Real Variable

The question used to be: *"Can they supply the volume?"* In 2026, the question is: *"Can they supply the right volume, to the right location, within the right window?"* Manufacturers with regional distribution infrastructure — bonded warehouses, established logistics partners, regional service centers — command genuine procurement advantages.

### LONGi — #2: Technology as Strategy

LONGi's rise to co-leadership with Jinko is the story of a long bet paying off. While most of the industry doubled down on TOPCon, LONGi committed to BC (Back Contact) technology — a fundamentally different cell architecture that places all electrical contacts on the rear surface of the cell, eliminating shading losses from front metallization.

**2026 performance indicators:**
- H1 2025 shipments: **39.57 GW**
- HIBC module power output: **above 700W**
- BC mass-production efficiency: approaching **26%**
- Hi-MO 9 module efficiency: **24.8%**

**What BC actually means for project performance:**
In standard TOPCon modules, approximately 4–5% of the cell surface is shaded by front-side metallic contacts. BC eliminates this entirely. The result is measurable performance gain in real-world conditions — not just on spec sheets. For constrained rooftops, high-irradiance locations, or projects where per-panel yield is contractually important, this gap is financially significant.

**The LONGi procurement case:**
Optimal for projects where the highest achievable module efficiency is worth a price premium. Also strategically valuable for EPCs looking to differentiate their offering to clients — LONGi's BC technology provides a credible technical narrative that supports premium positioning.

## Technology Route Deep Dive: TOPCon vs BC vs HJT {#technology-comparison}

Understanding the technology behind the module is increasingly important for procurement optimization. Here is a structured comparison of the three principal routes in 2026:

| Dimension | TOPCon (n-type) | BC (Back Contact) | HJT (Heterojunction) |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------------|---------------------|
| Mass-production efficiency | 24–25% | 25–26%+ | 24–25% |
| Cost per Watt (relative) | Baseline | +15–25% premium | +10–20% premium |
| Temperature coefficient | -0.30% to -0.35%/°C | ~-0.28%/°C | -0.24% to -0.26%/°C |
| Bifacial performance | Good | Not applicable | Excellent |
| Shading tolerance | Standard | Superior (no front metallization) | Standard |
| LID/LeTID degradation | Very low (n-type) | Very low | Very low |
| Best use case | General utility & C&I | Space-constrained, premium efficiency | High-temp, bifacial, specialty apps |
| Leading manufacturers | Jinko, JA, Trina, Tong | LONGi | Risen, Astronergy (partial) |

**Decision guidance:**
- **Default choice for most projects:** TOPCon n-type — best cost-efficiency ratio with proven scale
- **Premium efficiency requirement:** BC (LONGi HIBC) — pay the premium only when constrained area or per-panel contractual guarantees justify it
- **Bifacial + high-temperature environments:** HJT — particularly for floating solar, desert installations, and locations with significant diffuse light

## 2026 Procurement Trends Worth Watching {#procurement-trends}

**Trend 1: Framework Agreements Return**
Spot buying dominated 2023–2024 as prices fell consistently. With price stabilization, 2026 is seeing a return to 2–3 year framework agreements that lock pricing and reserve capacity. Buyers who establish these now gain a structural cost advantage if prices rise further.

**Trend 2: Supply Chain Transparency as Contract Clause**
European EPCs are increasingly embedding supply chain transparency requirements directly into module purchase contracts — IECQ, SMETA audits, and UFLPA compliance documentation. Manufacturers without this documentation are losing European bids regardless of price.

**Trend 3: AI-Assisted Performance Prediction**
Advanced developers are moving beyond standard yield simulation to incorporate manufacturer-specific performance decay curves, real-world bifacial gain data from deployed projects, and climate-specific LID models. Module selection is becoming a data science exercise.

**Trend 4: Warranty Insurance Standardization**
As the 25–30 year warranty commitments from manufacturers raise legitimate questions about counterparty risk, third-party warranty insurance (underwritten by Lloyd's, Munich Re, and similar) is becoming a standard procurement requirement on projects above €5M.

## Frequently Asked Questions {#faq}

**Who are the top 10 solar panel manufacturers in 2026?**
The global top 10 in 2026 are: Jinko Solar, LONGi, Trina Solar, JA Solar, Tongwei, Canadian Solar, DMEGC, Astronergy, Risen Energy, and Qcells. The leading group has remained relatively stable, with differentiation intensifying within tiers rather than reshuffling between them.

**Is TOPCon better than PERC in 2026?**
Yes, decisively. TOPCon n-type modules consistently outperform PERC across efficiency (by ~1–2%), degradation (lower LID), and temperature performance. In 2026, PERC has been effectively replaced by TOPCon as the mainstream production route. If you are evaluating new project supply, insist on n-type modules.

**What is the difference between TOPCon and HJT solar panels?**
Both are n-type technologies with low degradation. TOPCon uses tunnel oxide passivation contacts — it's the mainstream route with better cost. HJT uses amorphous silicon heterojunction layers — it achieves lower temperature coefficients and superior bifacial performance, at a cost premium. For hot climates or bifacial ground-mount applications, HJT's performance advantage often justifies the price difference.

**How do I verify a solar panel manufacturer's reliability?**
Check: (1) Bloomberg NEF or IECQ Tier 1 classification, (2) third-party verified warranty claims history, (3) installed project references from EPCs who used their products, (4) financial reports for margin and cash flow health, (5) regional distribution and technical support infrastructure. AvaliaSolar aggregates all these signals into a single Trust Score, with data contributed by verified EPCs and developers.

**Will solar panel prices rise or fall in 2026?**
The 2024 price floor appears to have been a cyclical low. European prices moved above that floor in early 2026. The consensus view among major procurement advisors is that prices will stabilize in a higher range than 2024, without returning to significant deflation. This makes 2026 a rational time to lock in multi-year supply agreements at current levels.

**What is BC (Back Contact) technology and why does it matter?**
BC cells place all electrical contacts on the rear surface of the cell, eliminating the front metallization that shades approximately 4–5% of the cell surface in conventional modules. The result is higher efficiency (LONGi's HIBC reaches 700W+ with near-26% mass production efficiency) and better shading tolerance. The trade-off is a meaningful price premium over TOPCon. BC makes economic sense for space-constrained projects, premium installations, or where per-panel yield is contractually specified.
